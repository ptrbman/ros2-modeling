Camera Case Example (S1T1R1H1)
==============================

System Topology (ASCII)
-----------------------

  [CAM_STD_1] --\
  [CAM_STD_2] ---+-> [OBJDET_1] --\
  [CAM_TH_1]  --/                  \
                                      +-> [FUSION_1] -> [ACTUATOR_1]
  [CAM_RP_1] --\                  /
  [CAM_HD_1] ---+-> [OBJDET_2] --/
  [CAM_HD_2]  --/


This case models a camera-driven robotics perception-and-control pipeline
in which camera streams are grouped before global decision making. A
camera publishes frames, an object-detection group processes one or more
camera streams, fusion combines outputs from one or more object-detection
groups, and the actuator consumes the fused state to trigger control
decisions. The experiment target is end-to-end timeliness and quality
under contention, rather than single-stream behavior in isolation.

The camera population is intentionally heterogeneous and uses four
predefined classes: standard, thermal, rapid, and hd. Multiple cameras
can be instantiated per class. Using the current repository
parameterization: standard is a balanced baseline
(period 220 ms, camera WCET 12 ms, objdet WCET 24 ms, chance 95,
deadline 700 ms, prio 2); thermal is slower and heavier with lower
confidence (period 800 ms, WCETs 40/80 ms, chance 75,
deadline 2400 ms, prio 4); rapid is the fastest and highest-confidence
stream (period 90 ms, WCETs 6/14 ms, chance 98, deadline 320 ms,
prio 1); and hd is medium-fast with heavier compute
(period 180 ms, WCETs 20/42 ms, chance 90, deadline 700 ms, prio 3).
These classes produce asynchronous arrivals and mixed criticality in
shared queues.

A concrete baseline uses one camera from each class (S1T1R1H1), but the
same topology supports larger compositions (for example, multiple
standard and hd cameras) and mixed-class routing into shared object
detection groups such as OBJDET_1 and OBJDET_2. This composition is
useful for queue-policy comparisons because bursts from rapid and hd
streams can interfere with thermal or standard processing depending on
dropping and priority rules, especially when categories share the same
OBJDET group. As an experiment template, it supports controlled sweeps
over queue policy, fusion period, grouping, and priority assignments
while keeping the overall architecture fixed, making differences in
deadline satisfaction, drop dynamics, and actuation throughput easier to
attribute to scheduling and buffering choices.
